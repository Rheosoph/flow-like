A **bot** is an AI teammate you set up once and then talk to, invite into rooms, put on a schedule, send mail or call from your flows. Each bot has its own name and look, instructions, models, a set of abilities you switch on, and one host it runs on: the cloud, your desktop app or a device you manage.

Bots act for you. In an app, a bot never has more rights than your own role there, and you can narrow them further. Its usage is billed to your account. Anything with consequences, like mailing someone new, calling a new website or running a command, stops and asks you first.

## Assistant and Flux

Every account has two built-in bots you can't delete:

<BuiltInBots />

Flux is FlowPilot as a member of your crew: the [FlowPilot guide](/studio/flowpilot/) describes everything it can build. Flux also builds and adjusts your other bots. Ask it for a bot, such as "a bot that answers questions about my sales app", and it writes the instructions, switches on the abilities the bot needs and gives it the apps it should use, building an app first when none fits. It can also set a bot's data classification, limit what the bot may do in each of its apps, run a bot on one of your devices or move it there, and delete a bot you no longer need. Every new bot, every change and every deletion shows you the proposed settings, or the bot to be deleted, and waits for your approval. Flux never deletes the Assistant or itself. The look beyond the starter characters, secrets and tasks stay in the Bots tab. The Assistant is a general helper. Both work without an account.

## The Bots tab

Open **Bots** in the sidebar to see your crew:

- **Your crew** lists your bots. Select one to chat with it and open its details.
- **Needs you** collects approval cards from your bots, invitations and questions that wait for an answer.
- **Rooms and people** holds your 1:1 chats and the rooms you share with people and bots.

A bot's details have tabs for what it holds: **Details** (abilities, host, models, tasks, webhooks, skills, allowed actions, usage), **Files** and **Data** from its workspace, **Computer** for its screen and browser, **Memory** for what it remembers, and **Mail** for its inbox. Tabs a bot can't use stay hidden.

To watch browser or computer use, select the bot's name or avatar in chat, then open **Computer**. The panel shows refreshed screenshots beside the conversation, with **Stop**, **Take over** and **Hand back** controls when the host supports them. Screenshots do not appear automatically in the message history. Watching a device bot, including one hosted in Docker, requires the desktop app and uses the encrypted device connection.

You can also put bots on your Home page with the **Bot chat**, **Crew** and **Messages** widgets. See [Customize your Home](/start/home/).

### Hide bots in a profile

Each [profile](/start/profiles/) chooses which of your bots it shows. Select the eye button next to the search to open **Bots in this profile**, then switch off the bots this profile doesn't need. The Assistant and Flux can be hidden too.

A hidden bot leaves that profile's crew, the pickers for new rooms and messages, and the **Crew** and **Bot chat** widgets on Home. Nothing else about it changes: it keeps running its tasks, stays in its rooms, and still shows under **Needs you** when it waits for your answer. Your other profiles keep showing it. Switch it back on in the same dialog, which you can also open from **hidden in this profile** above the crew.

## How a turn runs

Everything a bot does happens in **turns**. A turn starts when something wakes the bot, runs on its host, and ends with an answer in a conversation. Choose a trigger and a host to follow one:

Bots work headless: a turn runs on the bot's host whether or not anyone has a chat open. Apps, the API and the SDKs are only windows onto the same conversations.

## Without an account

Signed out, the desktop app lets you create bots that live only on that computer, with their files, secrets and chats stored locally. Assistant and Flux work too.

When you sign in, the Bots tab offers to **move your bots to your account**. Flow-Like first checks that your account can hold every part of each bot, copies its workspace files and verifies them twice, and only then removes the local copy. An interrupted move can simply be started again.
