It's 21:14 on the last train home. A customer reply is due before morning, the Wi-Fi icon is a sad gray X — and the draft still gets written, on your laptop, by a model that lives there. By the end of this course you'll run that exact setup: one gear for offline drafting, one gear with a hosted heavyweight for production. Both gears are *profiles*, and a profile is the first thing Flow-Like ever asks you about.

## 1 · What's in the box

@CourseBanner

The course banner is the whole plan in one picture: a brain-on-a-chip on the left, dotted lines running to a stack of model cards, and the one card with the green check flowing onward into a profile card with a person icon. That's the motion you'll practice all course long — pick models, put them in a profile, let the profile carry them everywhere.

A profile bundles the preferences that should change together: which models are assigned, your interests, your theme, and your FlowPilot model settings. Every flow you run reaches into the active profile for its AI toolkit. Change the profile and you change the toolkit — without touching a single flow.

Two things a profile is *not*:

- It's not an account. Profiles work without signing in; signing in adds account-backed settings and restores your profiles on another device.
- It's not a container for your apps. Switching profiles changes the active configuration — it never moves or duplicates your apps.

## 2 · Pick your first gear

If you've already opened Flow-Like, you did this without knowing the word for it.

@SelectingFirstProfile

The first-run screen — "Welcome to Flow-Like — Select your starting profile" — offers four starter cards: Chatting, Gaming, Learning, and Coding, plus a sign-in box for restoring profiles from another device. In the screenshot, Chatting is selected with an orange border and wears a LOCAL MODEL DOWNLOAD badge; the bar at the bottom shows the price of that badge: a 2.0 GiB download.

Chatting — "a thoughtful setup for conversation, writing", says the card — is a fine default. The screen itself makes the stakes low: choose one or more profiles that match your interests; you can always add, change, or remove profiles later. Select and hit **Download**.

## 3 · Let the download ride

@LoadingFirstModels

"Great Selection! Let's download your models — 43.75% finished." A red line chart tracks download speed, with badges reading 2.0 GiB Total and 640.0 MiB/s. You don't have to watch it: the orange **Background Download** button drops you into the app while the model files keep arriving.

That 2.0 GiB is a local model being copied onto your machine — the seed of your offline gear. Two minutes after launching the app for the first time, you have a working profile, a landing page, and a model on the way. That's your quick win: you already own a functioning AI toolkit, before you've built anything at all.

The rest of the course teaches you to stock it deliberately: read a model card (next lesson), weigh local against hosted, pick the right model *type* for each job, and finally run two gears side by side.

Recap:

- A profile bundles model assignments, interests, theme, and FlowPilot settings — the toolkit every flow reaches into.
- Switching profiles changes configuration; it never moves or duplicates your apps.
- First run: pick a starter profile, then let the download ride in the background.
