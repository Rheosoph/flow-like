It's Monday morning and your support inbox holds forty unanswered requests. Over this course you'll see how a Flow-Like app — call it the **Customer Support Copilot** — listens for each request, drafts a reply with AI, waits for a human thumbs-up, and sends the answer. Before you can read or build any of that, you need exactly four words: **Profile**, **App**, **Flow**, and **Studio**. That's the entire map, and by the end of this lesson you'll have walked it once in the product.

> **Predict first:** You build the copilot, then switch to a different Profile to try other model settings. Is the copilot still there when you arrive?

## 1 · Start at Home

@HomeOverview

Home greets you with one question: "What do you want to build?" The glowing bubble in the middle is **FlowPilot** — ask it in plain language and it creates apps, finds packages, or navigates for you. Shortcut chips float around it: *Create a new app*, *Switch my profile*, *Show me the package store*. The left sidebar is your compass: Home, FlowPilot, Explore, **My Apps**, University — and in the bottom corner sits the active Profile control (in the screenshot it reads "Offline · Anonymous").

## 2 · The four words

**Profile** — a bundle of preferences that change together: model assignments, interests, theme, FlowPilot settings. Switching Profiles swaps the active configuration and nothing else. Which resolves the prediction: yes, the copilot is still exactly where you left it. Apps don't belong to Profiles, and switching never moves or copies one.

**App** — the project boundary. Everything the copilot needs lives inside one App: its workflows, its chat surface, its data, its team settings.

@AppAnatomy

The diagram shows the shape directly: on the left, the Library card lists your apps; in the middle sits the App — Customer Support Copilot, "a project boundary for everything needed to build and ship an experience" — and five capability cards hang off it: **Flows**, **Experiences**, **Data**, **Reuse**, and **Delivery**. You'll meet each card when you need it. For now, remember the geometry: one App, many capabilities.

**Flow** — executable logic inside an App: a directed graph of typed nodes. The copilot might grow three Flows over time — one that answers requests, one that summarizes conversations, one that files follow-up tasks.

**Studio** — where you edit one Flow at a time. The graph on that canvas isn't a diagram *about* the program. It *is* the program the runtime executes.

## 3 · Offline or online

Flow-Like Desktop runs both kinds of App. An **offline** App lives on your device and needs no sign-in — perfect for experiments and personal automation. An **online** App uses a backend and adds browser access, collaboration, and publication. You'll make this choice for real in lesson 5; nothing in this course requires an account.

## Take the sixty-second walk

Do this now, in the product:

1. Open **Home** and find the Profile control in the bottom-left corner.
2. Click **My Apps** — this is your Library. Empty is fine; you'll fill it in lesson 5.
3. Open **Settings** — product preferences live here, apart from any project.
4. Return to **Home**. You've now touched every place this course will send you.

> **Watch out:** the one mistake everyone makes in week one is expecting a Profile switch to carry Apps along. It never does. Profiles configure *you*, not your projects.

## Recap

- Profile = your preferences. App = the project. Flow = the executable graph. Studio = the flow editor.
- Apps live in My Apps (your Library). Offline stays on this device; online adds browser and team.
- Next: you'll open the copilot's board in Studio and learn to read its two kinds of wires.
